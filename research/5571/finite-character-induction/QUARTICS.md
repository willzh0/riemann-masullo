# The minimal quartic examples

Consider a finite sum of finite-order primitive Hecke zero divisors over one number field $K$. Extend its coefficients by zero to all remaining characters. Call the coefficients invariant if every automorphism of $K/\mathbb Q$ preserves them. This qualifier excludes a difference which just subtracts a row from a field conjugate of that row. All statements about minimal degree here concern identities of the **full divisors**, including the central sites. The [divisor criterion](DIVISORS.md) allows these questions to be decided in finite group theory.

## A quartic field with trivial automorphism group

This is the specialization $(a,b)=(-1,1)$ of de Smit's pair of arithmetically equivalent octic fields. We use the two relative quadratic characters on the same quartic base to build the admissible test. [De Smit, *Generating arithmetically equivalent number fields with elliptic curves*, Proposition 1 and §3, pp. 394–396](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~smitbde/papers/ants1998-desmit.pdf#page=5)

Take $E:y^2=x^3-x+1$ over $\mathbb Q$ and $L=\mathbb Q(E[3])$. The four $x$-coordinates of the nonzero order-three points up to sign are the roots of
$$
 f(X)=3X^4-6X^2+12X-1 .
$$
For one such root $x$, put $K=\mathbb Q(x)$ and $D=x^3-x+1$. The polynomial modulo 5 is irreducible and modulo 7 has factor degrees 1 and 3. The projective Galois action on the four lines therefore contains a 4-cycle and a 3-cycle, and is $S_4$. A lift of a 4-cycle in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)$ lies in the nonsplit torus $\mathbb F_9^\times$ and has order eight. Its fourth power is $-I$, so the full Galois group is $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)$. The calculations can also be checked by enumerating the 48 matrices. [MIT elliptic curves notes, Lecture 5 on division polynomials](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-783-elliptic-curves-spring-2021/resources/mit18_783s21_notes5/) · [Finite verification](check_quartic.py)

In a basis with first coordinate the chosen line, $H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is the upper Borel. It has a unique common invariant line, so $N_G(H)=H$ and $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb Q}(K)=1$. On an upper triangular matrix with diagonal $(a,d)$, let the two signs be $a$ and $d$ under $\mathbb F_3^\times=\{1,-1\}$. The extension cut out by $a$ is $K(\sqrt D)$, since this records the sign of the $y$-coordinate. The determinant character cuts out $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ by the Weil pairing. Since $d=a\det$, the second quadratic extension is $K(\sqrt{-3D})$.

The induced trace on any matrix $g$ is the sum over its invariant lines of the character on that line (first row), or on its one-dimensional quotient (second row). A scalar or Jordan block gives identical values. With distinct rational eigenvalues the two fixed lines interchange the two contributions. A nonsplit element fixes no line. Hence the two induced characters coincide on all of $G$. Artin induction gives the same ordinary Hecke function at every rational prime, grouping the primes of $K$ above it. The two standard completed functions agree as well by the local data below, or by the equality of the induced representations at infinity and of their Artin conductors. The two quadratic extensions are nonisomorphic over $\mathbb Q$: the corresponding vector and covector kernels are not conjugate and both have trivial normal core. [Artin's induction identity, formula (18)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02941010) · [Group and Euler-polynomial check](check_quartic.py)

At the degree-one prime $(41,x-24)$ one has $D=25\bmod41$ and $-3D=7\bmod41$. These are respectively a square and a nonsquare. In particular $\psi_a-\psi_d$ is a nonzero angular function. On $C_K$ take $(\psi_a-\psi_d)h(\log|x|)$, with the normalized even profile $h$ constructed in the [divisor note](DIVISORS.md). Both characters have order two, so the test is real and even under inversion. It vanishes on $C_K^1$. Its two Mellin samples are opposite and nonzero throughout $0<|\Re z|<1/2$. Equality of the two completed functions cancels these samples with the full multiplicity of each matching zero, at every width, even if one exterior wing is read alone. This holds for every continuous norm section.

### The ramified primes and the two real places

The finite conductor of each quadratic character is the unique prime of $K$ over 3, of norm 3. The following calculation identifies the bad Euler factors directly.

- At 3, $1/x$ satisfies the Eisenstein polynomial $Y^4-12Y^3+6Y^2-3$. In the normalized valuation $v(D)=-3$ and $v(-3D)=1$. Both quadratic extensions are tamely ramified with conductor exponent one. The rational Euler factor is one.
- At 2 put $\lambda=x-1$. The coefficients of $f(1+\lambda)$, constant first, are $8,12,12,12,3$. The Newton slopes are $-1$ and $-2/3$, giving a linear factor and a totally ramified cubic. Both residue fields are $\mathbb F_2$. On the linear branch $D\equiv1\bmod8$. On the cubic branch $v(\lambda)=2$ and $v(2)=3$, and $D-x^2=\lambda^2(2+\lambda)$. Consequently $U=(D/x^2-1)/4$ is a unit. Writing a square root of $D/x^2$ as $1+2Z$ gives $Z^2+Z-U=0$, irreducible and separable in the residue field. Thus the character for $D$ splits at the first prime and is unramified nonsplit at the second. Multiplication by $-3$ interchanges the two signs, since $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt{-3})$ is unramified quadratic and both residue degrees are one. Their common rational Euler factor is $(1-2^{-2s})^{-1}$.
- At 23 one has $f(X)=3(X-7)(X-13)^3\bmod23$ and $v_{23}(f(13))=1$. The two completions are again linear and totally ramified cubic. At the simple root $D(7)=15\bmod23$ is a nonsquare. At the cubic root put $\lambda=x-13$. The expansion $D=2185+506\lambda+39\lambda^2+\lambda^3$ gives $D/\lambda^2=39=16\bmod\lambda$, a nonzero square. Hensel's lemma applies. Since $-3$ is a nonsquare modulo 23, multiplication by $-3$ interchanges these two signs. The common rational Euler factor is $(1-23^{-2s})^{-1}$.
- Away from $2,3,23$, the resultant $\operatorname{Res}(f,X^3-X+1)=-23^2$ shows that both radicands are units. The extensions are unramified there. The polynomial discriminant is $-27\cdot368^2$. The local degrees and tame different exponents just calculated give $d_K=-2^2 3^3 23^2=-57132$, so each induced rational conductor is $171396$.
- The field has two real embeddings and one conjugate pair. Its real roots lie in $(-3,-2)$ and $(0,1)$, where $D$ has opposite signs. Multiplication by $-3$ reverses the two real parities. Each standard archimedean factor is $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)^2\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s+1)^2$.

## Why degree four is minimal

Use the setup $G,H$ of the [divisor criterion](DIVISORS.md). If $K/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, $H$ is normal and the restriction of $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\psi$ to $H$ is the sum of the conjugates over $G/H$. On invariant coefficients this restriction multiplies the original class function by $[K:\mathbb Q]$. Distinct linear characters of $H$ are linearly independent. Thus there is no nonzero invariant full-divisor relation in any Galois field.

Here is the other case needed in degrees up to four. Suppose the faithful permutation group of the normal closure on the embeddings of $K$ is primitive and Frobenius with nontrivial point stabilizer. Let $C=\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}$. Each $\lambda=\psi|_C$ is $H$-fixed. Since $H$ is maximal and self-normalizing in $G$, the stabilizer of $\lambda$ is either $G$ or $H$. Two distinct $H$-fixed linear characters of $C$ cannot be conjugate in $G$: in the second case a conjugacy relating them must normalize $H$. Restriction of the putative induced relation to $C$ is a sum of conjugacy-orbit sums of such $\lambda$. Independence of characters of $C$ forces the sum of the coefficients in each fixed restriction class to be zero. Hence $f=\sum c_i\psi_i$ vanishes on $C$. Every $h\in H\setminus C$ fixes exactly one coset in $G/H$ by the Frobenius property, so $(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G f)(h)=f(h)$. Thus $f$ also vanishes on $H\setminus C$, and independence on $H$ gives all $c_i=0$.

A nonnormal cubic has faithful action $S_3$ with a point stabilizer of order two. This is the Frobenius case just proved. Degrees one and two are Galois, so the first example above is minimal. Among quartics the regular $C_4,V_4$ and the primitive Frobenius $A_4$ cases are likewise excluded. The remaining normal-closure groups $D_4$ (of order eight) and $S_4$ both occur. [Classification of the quartic groups, Cohen–Diaz y Diaz–Olivier, §1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/4FAECFC2E214F43D49191EE75ADCD780/S0010437X02000520a.pdf/enumerating-quartic-dihedral-extensions-of-dollaropen-qdollar.pdf)

## A classical dihedral quartic

Take the positive real $t=\sqrt[4]{3}$, $K=\mathbb Q(t)$. The nonisomorphic fields $K(\sqrt t)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[8]{3})$ and $K(\sqrt{2t})=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[8]{48})$ have equal Dedekind zeta functions by the classical arithmetical-equivalence example. Dividing by $\zeta_K$ gives equality of the two primitive quadratic Hecke functions, including their nontrivial zero orders. The other automorphism of $K$ sends $t$ to $-t$. The angular function
$$
 (\psi_t+\psi_{-t}-\psi_{2t}-\psi_{-2t})/2
$$
is invariant under this automorphism. At the degree-one prime $(13,t-2)$ its value is $-2$, since $-1$ is a square and 2 is a nonsquare modulo 13. Multiplication by the same even radial profile yields a nonzero invariant test cancelling at each zero, on each exterior wing. [De Smit–Perlis, *Zeta functions do not determine class numbers*, p. 213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9410216#page=1) · [De Smit, *Brauer–Kuroda relations for S-class numbers*, §5](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/83708#page=10)
