# The fixed quartic prime deficit and its periodic dilation

Take the primitive quartic character modulo five with $\chi(2)=i$, and write $S_\chi(x)=\sum_p\chi(p)\log p\,(x-p)_+$. The unresolved arithmetic upper is $\sum_{j\le J}[-\Re S_\chi(j^4)]_+\le J^{7+o(1)}$ along an unbounded sequence. Mlyle proved that at $x=j^4$ this deficit can be computed, up to a stretched-log saving at critical scale, from a signed dilation involving smaller prime cutoffs $x/n\le x/2$. These results apply to that fixed character, with the true quartic values on the primes. [Mlyle’s arithmetic reduction](https://github.com/mlyle/riemann-masullo/blob/c2eb094fffef928fdb72c2e180c255092e819347/research/5571/fourth-power-prime-curvature/spectral-interpolation-periodic-prime-convolution.md) · [Every-prefix zero criterion](../fourth-power-deficit-boundary/README.md)

**A criterion using only primes up to $J$.** Put $K(u)=1-3u/2$ up to $u=1/2$, $K(u)=(1-u)^2/(2u)$ for $1/2<u\le1$, and zero beyond one. The exact signed prefix of $Q_\chi(j)=\sum_{p\le j}\chi(p)\log p\,K(p/j)$ has exponent $1+\Theta_\chi$ in either sign, where $\Theta_\chi$ is the supremum of the real parts of the quartic zeros. Each exterior zero at $\beta$ forces both signed sums $\gg J^{1+\beta}$ at every sufficiently large prefix. Thus a $J^{3/2+o(1)}$ upper along an unbounded sequence suffices for this fixed character. This is an actual fourth-power dilation with positive trapezoidal weights on $j^3\le n\le2j^3$. Its difference from $j^7Q_\chi(j)$ is a finite fractional-part correction of size $O(j^2)$. A smooth nonnegative annulus also works at every fixed power of $j^4$. [Finite prime formula, proof and exact dictionary](linear-prime-cutoffs.md)

**Smaller cutoffs at every prescribed terminal sample.** Möbius inversion eliminates all dilators through $Y=\lfloor\exp(\kappa\Phi(\log x))\rfloor$, where $\Phi(u)=u^{3/5}(\log u)^{-1/5}$ and $\kappa>0$ is sufficiently small. Each remaining cutoff is at most $x/(Y+1)$. The error is still $O(x^{3/2}e^{-c'\Phi(\log x)})$, including after any sample-dependent phase projection. The remaining terms retain signed correlations. The normalized filter has exact independent-error cost $(10/\pi^2+o(1))\sqrt Y$, and the squared coefficients of prime dilators alone sum to $(2/5+o(1))\log\log x$. A fixed strip for the quadratic companion gives power shortening. [Cutoff reduction and sharp norm](compression.md)

**The only period-five weight that can cancel at critical scale.** In the actual short row, replace the dilator value $\chi(n)$ by any five-periodic complex weight $a(n)$, allowing nonzero period mean. For even one fixed real phase, a one-sided $o(j^6)$ terminal bound or a one-sided $o(J^7)$ prefix forces $a=a(1)\chi$. Four genuine unpaired quartic zeros certify the rank with least singular value exceeding $0.85224$. For the centered canonical representatives $a(1)=0,\ \sum a=0,\ \|a\|_2=1$, both signed sums exceed $cJ^7$ on one fixed-ratio terminal shell for every large $J$, even if the entire residue weight and phase are chosen afresh at each prefix. Their optimized exponent is determined by the common kernel of the actual exterior residue evaluations. [Four-zero rigidity and numerical bounds](RIGIDITY.md) · [Adaptive centered weights](adaptive-weights.md)

**An observable critical floor at every large prefix of the original primes.** Take the minimum over one phase per prefix of $\sum_{j\le J}[\Re(e^{-i\phi}S_\chi(j^4))]_+$. Its growth exponent is exactly $5+4\Theta_\Delta$, where $\Theta_\Delta$ is the larger of $1/2$ and the supremum of real parts at which conjugate zero multiplicities differ. Each prefix has two-sided mass $\gg J^7$ on a fixed terminal shell, uniformly in that phase. Conjugating just the multiplicative dilator gives the corresponding exponent for zeros entirely absent from the conjugate divisor. Weighted logarithmic dilators and a relative phase also have explicit signed critical excursions. [Naked prime phase theorem](PHASE.md) · [Conjugate dilator](CROSSED.md) · [Logarithmic and phase-weighted probes](weighted-probes.md) · [Positive theta kernel for the same quartic](THETA.md)

**The remaining spectrum of the matched full dilation.** Writing $\eta=\chi^2$, the quartic poles disappear and the next poles come from $2s$ at a zero of $L(\cdot,\eta)$ and $3s$ at a zero of $L(\cdot,\bar\chi)$. Their multiplicities add at coincidences. The explicit formula has remainder $O(x^{6/5})$. At every fixed phase its values at $x=j^4$ have both signed excursions of size at least $0.0317j^5$ in the limsup sense, from a certified quadratic zero. Under the critical-line hypothesis for this quartic, the bound $O(j^5)$ for the full dilation is equivalent to the critical-line hypothesis for the quadratic companion. [Full spectrum and certificate](secondary-spectrum.md)

**What the physical size estimates permit.** A fixed density-one subset of genuine primes, retaining any prescribed finite start and all original residue labels and weights on its support, satisfies the measured dilation, signed local and curvature bounds while producing $-\Re S^*_\chi(j^4)\gg (j^4)^\nu$ infinitely often for any prescribed $\nu<8/5$. This changes the prime support. Its full identity uses the restricted log radical. For arbitrary physical prime coefficients of modulus at most $\log p$, a genuine zero-free half-plane $\Re s>b$ for $L(s,\chi)$ and a full dilation $O(x^{1+b})$ imply the cumulative absolute upper $O_\epsilon(J^{5+4b+\epsilon})$ and the pointwise upper $O_\epsilon(x^{(6+4b)/5+\epsilon})$. Thus the critical case has sharp pointwise exponent $8/5$ in that larger class. [Construction and recovery bounds](terminal-and-cumulative.md)

**Nonzero-mean weights have a larger, unavoidable signed deficit.** For every allowed smooth cutoff and every bounded period-five weight whose mean stays bounded away from zero, both signed sums are $\gg J^{7.42}$ on a fixed terminal shell for every sufficiently large $J$. The whole weight may vary with the prefix. Subtracting the continuous mean from the periodic dilator produces two genuine quartic spectra, at $\rho$ and $0.21+0.79\rho$. Two certified low frequencies force a projected coefficient exceeding $0.000617|\mu|$ for period mean $\mu$. For every power cutoff, each sufficiently large terminal shell has signed order at least $J^{7+2\kappa}$ for $N=x^\kappa,\ 0<\kappa<1$. The fixed-phase pointwise subsequence order is at least $j^{6+2\kappa}$. [Two-frequency proof for all power cutoffs](moving-mean.md) · [Every-large-shell theorem for every fixed power cutoff](moving-spectrum.md)
