# Divisors under a finite Galois norm

Let $L/F$ be finite Galois, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)$, $H=[G,G]$ and $E=L^H$. Let $\psi$ be any continuous unitary Hecke character on $F$. Class field theory identifies $\widehat{G^{\rm ab}}$ with exactly the characters of $C_F$ trivial on $N_{L/F}C_L$. Thus the full set with the same upstairs pullback as $\psi$ is $\{\psi\kappa:\kappa\in\widehat{G^{\rm ab}}\}$. At every $0<\Re\rho<1$ one has
$$
 m_L(\psi\circ N_{L/F},\rho)\ \ge\
       \sum_{\kappa\in\widehat{G^{\rm ab}}}m_F(\psi\kappa,\rho).
$$
For abelian $G$ this is an equality. This is the Artin–Hecke version of the classical Aramata–Brauer argument over the maximal abelian subfield. [Milne, global norm limitation, VIII, Theorem 4.8](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=251) · [Heilbronn, p. 871](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1973-090-3) · [Wong, Lemma 4.2, pp. 1561–1562](https://www-math.nsysu.edu.tw/~pjwong/stuff/Heilbronn.pdf)

## Positive induction

For a nontrivial cyclic subgroup $C$ of $H$ and $\eta\in\widehat C$ put
$$
 b(C,\eta)=\varphi(|C|)-\sum_{\langle c\rangle=C}\overline{\eta(c)}.
$$
It vanishes for $\eta=1$. For every nontrivial $\eta$ it is a positive integer. Indeed the second term is a Ramanujan sum and is real. At any generator a nontrivial $\eta$ has value different from 1, so its real part is strictly smaller than 1. Fourier orthogonality on $C$ gives
$$
 \sum_{\eta\in\widehat C}b(C,\eta)\eta(t)
   = |C|\big(\varphi(|C|)\mathbf1_{t=1}-\mathbf1_{\langle t\rangle=C}\big).
$$
Induce this function to $H$ and sum once over every distinct nontrivial cyclic subgroup. For $t\ne1$ and a conjugator $h$, exactly one subgroup is generated by $h^{-1}th$. At the identity use $\sum_{C\ne1}\varphi(|C|)=|H|-1$. This proves
$$
 |H|(\operatorname{Reg}_H-1_H)
   =\sum_{C\ne1}\sum_{\eta\ne1}b(C,\eta)\operatorname{Ind}_C^H\eta .
$$
Inducing to $G$ converts the character on the left, apart from the multiplier $|H|$, to the regular character of $G$ minus its full sum of one-dimensional characters. The argument includes $H=1$, when both sides vanish. [Aramata, Sätze 4–6, pp. 32–34](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/pjab1912/9/2/9_2_31/_pdf/-char/en#page=2) · [Brauer, p. 244](https://doi.org/10.2307/2371849)

Tensor with $\psi$ and apply multiplicativity and induction for Artin–Hecke functions. Each term on the right becomes the Hecke function on $L^C$ of $(\psi\circ N_{L^C/F})\eta$, using reciprocity for $L/L^C$. At an open-strip site the resulting identity of orders is
$$
 |H|\left(m_L(\psi\circ N_{L/F},\rho)-
       \sum_\kappa m_F(\psi\kappa,\rho)\right)
     =\sum_{C\ne1}\sum_{\eta\ne1} b(C,\eta)
          m_{L^C}((\psi\circ N_{L^C/F})\eta,\rho).
$$
Every order on the right is nonnegative. All these statements concern the true completed functions, including when two twists cancel each other's finite ramification. For an interior divisor proof it suffices to compare the Euler products off the finitely many bad primes. A removed unitary local factor is a product of $(1-\alpha q^{-s})^{-1}$ with $|\alpha|=1$ on its invariant subspace and is a holomorphic unit for $\Re s>0$. The unitary archimedean factors and the project's changed archimedean constants are holomorphic units in the open strip. For an exact local identity use the inertia invariants of the tensor product. [Tate, §§2.3.5 and 3.1–3.3, pp. 12–14](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/TateNumberTheory.pdf#page=12) · [Classical Artin–Hecke induction](https://www-math.nsysu.edu.tw/~pjwong/stuff/Heilbronn.pdf)

In fact every factor on the right of the product identity is entire for the standard completion. If one were the Hecke function of a pure norm character, write $(\psi\circ N_{L^C/F})\eta=|\cdot|^{it}$. On pulling up to $L$ the $\eta$ disappears. Norm limitation then places $\psi|\cdot|^{-it}$ in $\widehat{G^{\rm ab}}$. Its restriction to $C\le[G,G]$ is trivial, which forces $\eta=1$. For nonabelian $G$ a positive factor occurs, and the usual zero count yields infinitely many strict inequalities in the whole open strip for each fixed $\psi$. This gives no location off the critical line. The surplus counted for $|\Im\rho|\le T$ has leading term
$$
 \frac{[F:\mathbb Q](|G|-|G^{\rm ab}|)}{\pi}\,T\log T+O_{L,\psi}(T),
$$
by subtracting the ordinary two-sided Hecke zero counts for the finite set of functions in the quotient.

## The splitting field of the cube root of two

For $F=\mathbb Q$ and $L$ the splitting field of $X^3-2$, $G=S_3$, $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and $H=C_3$. Choose a nontrivial character $\theta$ of $H$. The two cubic characters over $E$ are interchanged by conjugation over $\mathbb Q$ and their common rational twist on $E$ stays fixed under this conjugation. The exact open-strip identity becomes
$$
 m_L(\psi\circ N_{L/\mathbb Q},\rho)
 =m_{\mathbb Q}(\psi,\rho)+m_{\mathbb Q}(\psi\chi_{-3},\rho)
         +2m_E((\psi\circ N_{E/\mathbb Q})\theta,\rho).
$$
The two rational rows merge on pullback to $L$, with both multiplicities present. The remaining term illustrates why replacing a nonabelian extension by an abelian product equality can lose zeros.
