# Every bicyclic field of square degree attains the bound

Let $F/\mathbb Q$ be **any fixed Galois field with group $A=C_n\times C_n$**, $n\ge2$. There is a finite-order Hecke character $\lambda$ on this very field whose ordinary Hecke function is an exact $n$th power of a single finite-order Hecke function on a degree-$n$ subfield, with the same identity for each rational twist. The character $\lambda$ can be chosen invariant under all of $A$. The base field can therefore be prescribed in advance within this class. These fields exist for every $n$, for example by taking cyclic degree-$n$ subfields at two different prime cyclotomic conductors congruent to $1\bmod 2n$.

Here is a description of **all** invariant continuous unitary characters on $F$, modulo rational norm pullbacks. Write $I_A C_F$ for the subgroup generated by $a(x)x^{-1}$, $a\in A$, $x\in C_F$. It is closed. Indeed $C_F^1$ is compact, and one can make a positive norm section $s_0$ invariant by using $\prod_{a\in A} a(s_0(t^{1/|A|}))$ at $t>0$. Each augmentation difference then comes from $C_F^1$, and the full subgroup is a product of finitely many compact subgroups. By global class formation and Tate's theorem,
$$
 \ker(N:C_F\to C_{\mathbb Q})/I_A C_F
    =\widehat H^{-1}(A,C_F)
    \cong \widehat H^{-3}(A,\mathbb Z)
    =H_2(A,\mathbb Z)
    \cong \bigwedge\nolimits^2 A
    \cong C_n .
$$
For two cyclic factors the computation of $H_2$ follows also from the product formula in group homology and $H_2(C_n,\mathbb Z)=0$. A character of this finite subgroup of the locally compact quotient $C_F/I_A C_F$ extends to a continuous circle-valued character by Pontryagin duality. The norm has open finite-index image. Dualizing shows that the group $X$ of $A$-invariant unitary characters modulo rational norm pullbacks is cyclic of exact order $n$. [Tate, *The Higher Dimensional Cohomology Groups of Class Field Theory*, pp. 294–297](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969801) · [Takahashi, *Homology groups in class field theory*, p. 8](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tmj1949/5/1/5_1_8/_pdf/-char/en) · [Milne, II, theorem 3.11 and VIII, theorem 4.7](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=251)

Such an invariant character is a finite-order character times a common imaginary norm twist. Indeed the infinite places of $F$ constitute one orbit, so its real norm exponent is the same at every place in the normalized local absolute values. At a complex place the decomposition group contains conjugation, which forces angular weight zero. After removing the common norm exponent it is trivial on the connected infinite factors. Its finite local units have finite image since a continuous homomorphic image of a profinite group in the circle is a compact profinite subgroup of the circle. The character then factors through a finite ray class group. In particular every class in $X$ has a finite-order representative.

Choose a direct cyclic factor $B\subset A$, put $E=F^B$, let $\kappa$ be a character generating $\widehat B$, and fix a generator $\sigma$ of $A/B$. By the [cyclic descent lemma](DESCENT.md), each $A$-invariant $\lambda$ can be written $\nu\circ N_{F/E}$. Its possible lifts are the $\nu\kappa^r$. All lifts are finite-order if $\lambda$ is, since $N(C_F)$ has finite index. As $\lambda$ is fixed, $\nu^\sigma/\nu$ kills $N(C_F)$ and equals $\kappa^j$ for some $j\bmod n$. The character $\kappa$ is fixed by $\sigma$, by naturality of reciprocity and the commutativity of $A$. Thus $j$ is independent of the chosen lift. The association is a homomorphism on $X$. Its kernel is trivial: if $j=0$, then $\nu$ descends further along the cyclic extension $E/\mathbb Q$. Conversely every rational norm pullback has $j=0$. Since $|X|=n$, this labels all $n$ invariant classes.

With $g=\gcd(j,n)$ and $e=n/g$, the $n$ lower factors split into $g$ orbits under $\sigma$, each of size $e$. Conjugate characters of $E$ have equal ordinary Hecke functions as complex functions since the ideals and the local factors are permuted. Therefore
$$
 L_F(s,\lambda)
  =\left(\prod_{r=0}^{g-1}L_E(s,\nu\kappa^r)\right)^e .
$$
This is an equality of ordinary functions and hence of open-strip zero divisors. A rational twist multiplies the bottom characters by its norm pullback and preserves $j$. For a generator of $X$, $g=1$: there is a single $n$th power, and $\nu$ has trivial stabilizer on $E$. Its positive orders are therefore all multiplied by exactly $n$. The same $\lambda$ gives a one-factor identity over **each** cyclic direct-factor fixed field, since the map from $X$ in each case is an isomorphism. This says nothing about whether a particular lower zero is simple.

For $n=p$ prime there is one rational norm-pullback class and $p-1$ classes giving pure $p$th powers. For $p=3$, the two nontrivial classes and their root characters can be identified using specific defining polynomials and local Frobenius data. [Degree-nine calculation](NONIC.md)

For each fixed $F$ in this family, an order bound from #7220 must therefore be at least $n=\sqrt{[F:\mathbb Q]}$. The sharpness proof concerns exact full Hecke-divisor powers. The [optimality argument](POWERS.md) bounds this particular arithmetic mechanism for any finite-order character over any field. It leaves possible additional analytic multiplicities open.
